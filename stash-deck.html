Stash

Your reading should compound. Stash turns everything you save into a knowledge base you can actually think with.

Devika · Personal Knowledge Management

01 — The Problem

We save more than ever.
We retain almost nothing.

01
The Graveyard
You bookmark an article with every intention. It disappears into a folder you never open again. Saving feels productive. It isn't.
02
The Tab Trap
You leave tabs open to keep things visible. By month end: 40+ tabs, no memory of why you opened any of them.
03
The Scramble
You read something brilliant last week. Someone asks today. You search, fail, re-Google. Your reading didn't stick.

"I read a lot — but when I need to recall something, I have nothing. My reading doesn't compound."

03 — The Insight

"Existing tools are built for archiving.
Stash is built for thinking."

Pocket, Raindrop, and Notion optimize for organization at save-time. But you don't know why you'll need something when you save it. The product must be built around retrieval, not storage.

Old Model
Save → Archive
→
Stash Model
Save → Think

04 — The Solution

A knowledge base that
works for you.

Save anything in one click. AI does all the organizing. Talk to your collection like a research assistant that only knows what you've read.

Step 01 — Capture
Save in one click
Chrome extension captures articles, YouTube videos, and PDFs. No folders. No manual tags. Zero friction at the moment of saving.
Chrome Extension
Step 02 — Organize
AI does the work
Every save is automatically summarized, tagged, and categorized by Gemini. Organized before it hits your library — without you touching it.
Gemini AI
Step 03 — Recall
Ask, don't search
"What did I save about attention spans?" Conversational queries over your personal knowledge base, with citations from your own saves.
Semantic Search
Step 04 — Retain
Your reading compounds
Weekly digests resurface saves. Spaced repetition reinforces key ideas. Your reading stops being a graveyard and starts building expertise.

07 — Why Now

The conditions are right

↑
Content volume is overwhelming
AI is generating more content faster than ever. Curation tools built in 2018 are breaking in 2025. The problem is getting worse, not better.
⚡
LLMs make semantic search cheap
Building a personal RAG system used to require serious ML infra. Today, Gemini's free tier makes it accessible to a solo builder.
🧩
Existing tools haven't evolved
Pocket (2007), Raindrop, Instapaper — all pre-LLM. None have rebuilt around semantic recall. The category is stale.
🎯
Personal AI is a shift, not a feature
Users now expect AI that knows their context. A knowledge base that's genuinely yours is where this space heads.
